## 2. Current positioning and voice

The current site positions Intimedev as a dependable, full-stack, long-term partner. Its strongest, most specific claims are:

1. **App + infra**: the same team writes the application and runs the server.
2. **Legacy OK**: they take over and modernise systems others walked away from.
3. **Long-term**: most client relationships span years.
4. **Three markets across time zones**: Egypt, Australia, UK.

The voice is plain and factual, with a few generic phrases ("real business growth", "innovative"). The visual identity is a common SaaS template: indigo accent, slate text, Manrope, rounded cards, a purple-gradient hourglass logo. Nothing in it would identify Intimedev with the logo removed.

## 3. Audience (Provisional, inferred from services and markets)

1. **Owner-operators and operations leads at small and mid-sized businesses in Australia and the UK** who run on a custom system (booking, membership, ERP, logistics) and need someone to build, host and maintain it. They value reliability, communication and continuity more than novelty.
2. **Companies with a legacy system** (old PHP or CakePHP apps, unmaintained servers) whose original developer is gone.
3. **Egyptian and regional businesses** that need ERP, e-commerce or e-learning platforms built and run locally.
4. **Agencies and startups** that need a back-end and infrastructure team behind their product (secondary).

## 4. Competitive landscape

Egyptian custom-software firms found in directories (Code Wealth, Web Pioneer, Raqmix, PS Solutions, Darory, Nile Bits) present near-identical offers: ERP, web, mobile, outsourcing. Their sites share the same visual vocabulary: blue or purple gradients, stock photos of laptops, icon-card grids, client-logo walls. Internationally, studios that stand out (Basement, Linear-adjacent product studios) win on one coherent idea executed everywhere, not on effects.

**Gap we can own:** nobody in this segment owns *time* (punctuality, continuity, time-zone overlap) as a design system, even though the company is literally named after it, and nobody connects an Egyptian engineering company to Egypt's own history of measuring time.

## 5. A verified story the brand can use

One of the oldest known sundials was found in the Valley of the Kings in 2013 by a University of Basel team: a 13th-century BC limestone fragment with a semicircle divided into twelve sections, and a hole for a peg that cast the shadow ([Archaeology, 2013](https://archaeology.org/news/2013/03/15/130315-egypt-valley-kings-workers-sundial/)). An Egyptian company called "In Time" can draw its graphic language from that object honestly, without claiming anything about itself.

## 6. Strategy

- **Positioning:** The Egyptian engineering team that builds your software on time and keeps it running for years: application, servers and support from one team.
- **Brand promise:** *Built on time. Kept running.*
- **Personality:** precise, steady, candid, quietly confident, hands-on. Not "disruptive", not playful.
- **Attributes:** punctual, accountable, technically complete (app to server), long-sighted, clear across time zones.
- **Tone:** short declarative sentences; specific nouns (servers, backups, invoices, bookings) over abstractions; numbers only when verified; no hype words (innovative, cutting-edge, seamless, next-gen).
- **Differentiation:**
  1. One team for code and infrastructure (most agencies hand hosting to someone else).
  2. Years-long maintenance and legacy rescue as a first-class offer, not an afterthought.
  3. Time-zone overlap with Australia and the UK shown concretely, not claimed.
  4. A visual identity rooted in Egyptian timekeeping, which no competitor can borrow.
